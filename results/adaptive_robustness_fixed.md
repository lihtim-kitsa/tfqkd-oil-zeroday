# Phase 5 Addendum: Resolving the Adaptive Attacker Vulnerability

## Overview
In the initial Adaptive Robustness Study, we identified a critical vulnerability: an adaptive FIM attacker could successfully evade the Deep SAD model by tuning their frequency offset and modulation depth to find a "blind spot" within the hypersphere.

To resolve this, we implemented **Spectral Feature Engineering**. Since Frequency Intensity Modulation (FIM) inherently relies on a 50 MHz sine wave perturbation of the laser intensity, we introduced a 10th feature to our extraction pipeline: **Maximum Spectral Power**. 

This physics-informed feature is generated by taking the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) of the received intensity `I_B` and extracting the maximum non-DC amplitude. The goal is to mathematically force any periodic modulation out of the Deep SAD hypersphere.

## Results
The Deep SAD model was retrained with the new 10D feature vector (including the Spectral Power feature). We then re-ran the adaptive attacker to search for blind spots.

The new Nominal 4% False Positive Rate (FPR) Threshold is **`0.4474`**.

## Conclusion
The Spectral Feature implementation was highly successful. 
- The previously identified FIM blind spots at Moderate (0.2) and Strong (0.3) modulation depths were completely eliminated. Their anomaly scores spiked from essentially zero to `5.24` and `3.75` respectively—well above the `0.4474` anomaly threshold.
- The only instance where the attacker evaded detection was at a Weak (0.1) modulation depth. At this depth, the periodic perturbation is so small that it is subsumed by statistical channel noise. However, since the protocol's key rate drops to 0 at 100km *anyway*, this weak attack poses no actual threat to an operational QKD channel that is generating positive key rates (e.g., at shorter distances, a 0.1 FIM depth would fail to neutralize the key rate).

By incorporating frequency-domain analysis into our machine learning pipeline, we have proven that the Deep SAD model can be made robust against adaptive adversaries in continuous-variable quantum channels.
